## 1. Purpose

This document is the sales-facing source of truth for the current VisuTry Store pilot motion.

It converts the current product and engineering direction into consistent merchant-facing language, outreach copy, demo narrative, pilot offer, objection handling, and qualification questions.

The sales rule is:

> **Sell the Storefront first because it is concrete and easy to understand. Expand the conversation into Campaign and AI Commerce value only after the merchant understands the core shopper workflow.**

Internal product direction remains:

> **Storefront is the delivery surface. AI Commerce / Campaign Engine is the business.**

These statements do not conflict.

- **Storefront** is the first SaaS product surface and first pilot unit.
- **Campaign** is the growth and usage expansion model around different traffic, audience, and intent contexts.
- **AI Commerce Infrastructure** is the long-term platform position.

## 2. What We Are Selling Now

### 2.1 External pilot positioning

Primary merchant-facing positioning:

> **Turn your eyewear catalog into a personalized AI shopping experience.**

Supporting explanation:

> VisuTry helps shoppers upload a selfie, receive recommendations from your own frame catalog, try selected frames on, compare finalists, and continue to your product or inquiry flow. You can also see which frames and shopper journeys show stronger purchase intent.

This is the default sales language for cold outreach, first calls, landing-page conversion, and the opening minutes of the demo.

### 2.2 What not to lead with

Do not open with:

- `AI Commerce Infrastructure`;
- `Campaign Engine`;
- `Agent-Ready Commerce`;
- `virtual try-on API`;
- `AI agent protocol`;
- model names or technical architecture.

These are second-layer or internal concepts. Leading with them increases explanation cost and makes the product sound broader than the current pilot offer.

### 2.3 Expansion positioning after comprehension

Once the merchant understands the Storefront, expand the story:

> You do not have to replace your current ecommerce site. The VisuTry experience can sit between your traffic and your existing product pages or checkout. You can use it for a dedicated campaign, social traffic, email, QR, paid media, or other high-intent entry points.

Then introduce the broader value:

> **Storefront is the shopper experience. Campaign makes that experience reusable for different traffic and audiences.**

Only where relevant, add:

> VisuTry is also being built so AI assistants and future shopping agents can understand merchant catalog facts and route attributable traffic into the same decision workflow.

## 3. Product Positioning Ladder

Use this hierarchy consistently.

| Layer | Merchant language | Business role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. AI Storefront | Personalized AI shopping experience for your eyewear catalog | First SaaS product / pilot surface |
| 2. Campaign Experience | Use the experience for specific traffic, audiences, collections, or offers | ARPU and usage expansion |
| 3. Commerce Intelligence | Understand recommendation, try-on, compare, product-click, and intent behavior | Merchant insight / optimization |
| 4. Agent-Ready Commerce | Make catalog and commerce surfaces understandable and attributable for AI-agent traffic | Future distribution expansion |
| 5. AI Commerce Infrastructure | One decision and conversion layer between traffic and merchant commerce | Long-term platform positioning |

The first merchant should be able to buy Layer 1 without buying into the full long-term vision.

## 4. Core Merchant Problem

Use merchant language, not product language.

### Primary problem

> Eyewear shoppers often browse many frames but still do not know which ones to try first or which ones suit them. That uncertainty creates hesitation before product click, inquiry, appointment, or purchase.

### Secondary problems

- generic VTO can show a frame but does not necessarily help the shopper decide which frame to choose;
- merchant catalogs are often difficult to navigate by face, style, or shopper intent;
- merchants see page views but often have limited visibility into which frames generated real interest;
- small and mid-size merchants do not want to build a full AI / AR commerce stack before proving value.

### VisuTry response

> VisuTry combines catalog intelligence, personalized recommendation, virtual try-on, comparison, and intent tracking into one merchant-branded decision journey.

## 5. ICP for the First Pilot Motion

Prioritize merchants with:

- online eyewear catalog or ecommerce site;
- approximately 20-500 active frames;
- usable product imagery;
- online purchase, inquiry, appointment, pre-shop, or social-selling workflow;
- meaningful traffic from Search, Social, Ads, Email, QR, Direct, or Referral;
- owner, ecommerce lead, or growth lead reachable directly;
- willingness to start with 8-20 representative frames;
- no requirement for medical-grade measurement or EHR/PMS integration to begin.

Preferred first segments:

1. Shopify-native DTC eyewear brands;
2. independent online eyewear sellers;
3. independent optical stores with an active website;
4. boutique eyewear brands using Instagram / TikTok / creator traffic.

Do not optimize initial outbound for enterprise chains or procurement-heavy accounts.

## 8. First Call Structure — 15 Minutes

### Minute 0-3 — qualify the current selling flow

Ask:

1. Where does most online traffic come from today?
2. Do shoppers buy online, inquire, book appointments, or mostly pre-shop before visiting?
3. How large is the active frame catalog?
4. How do shoppers currently decide which frames to try?
5. Do you use any virtual try-on today?

Do not immediately demo every feature.

### Minute 3-5 — frame the problem

Use:

> What we are trying to solve is not only “can the shopper see a frame on their face?” It is “how does the shopper get from a large catalog to a small set of confident choices?”

Then:

> VisuTry combines recommendation, try-on, compare, and merchant intent in one flow.

### Minute 5-12 — working demo

Follow the implemented Sales Demo flow:

1. merchant-branded entry;
2. shopper photo upload;
3. merchant-catalog recommendation;
4. shortlist and recommendation reason;
5. try-on;
6. compare;
7. product click / favorite / inquiry;
8. merchant insight.

The working demo already uses real merchant-scoped Store records, recommendation, Try-On, Compare, and intent/insight surfaces. Do not imply unsupported checkout, physical-fit measurement, or medical capability.

### Minute 12-15 — identify the first pilot use case

Ask:

> If you tested this next month, where would you send traffic from first?

Prompt only if needed:

- a dedicated link from the website;
- Instagram / TikTok traffic;
- paid ads;
- email campaign;
- QR in-store;
- pre-appointment / pre-shop flow;
- a featured frame collection.

Then ask:

> Which result would matter most to you: product clicks, inquiries, appointments, add-to-cart, conversion, or revenue?

This moves the discussion naturally from Storefront to Campaign without forcing the Campaign terminology.

## 9. 10-Minute Demo Talk Track

### 0-1 min — merchant problem

> Shoppers often browse many frames but do not know which ones are worth trying. VisuTry narrows your own catalog before the shopper buys or contacts your team.

### 1-3 min — recommendation

> This is your catalog, not a generic frame library. The shopper uploads one front-facing photo, and VisuTry uses shopper signals plus frame attributes to create a merchant-specific shortlist.

### 3-6 min — Try-On + Compare

> Recommendation is the starting point. The shopper can then visually validate the shortlist through Try-On and compare finalists side by side.

### 6-8 min — commerce intent

> Every recommended and tried frame stays connected to your product. The shopper can continue to the product, favorite it, or submit an inquiry depending on your selling flow.

### 8-10 min — merchant intelligence

> You can see more than raw traffic: which frames were recommended, tried, compared, favorited, or clicked, and which sessions showed stronger intent.

Close:

> We normally suggest starting with 8-20 representative frames and one real traffic source. That gives us enough to test whether this improves your shopper decision flow before doing a larger integration.

## 10. How to Introduce Campaign

Do not sell Campaign as a separate product in the first sentence.

Introduce it after asking where the merchant would deploy the Storefront.

### Natural transition

Merchant: “Where would I put this?”

Response:

> You can use the hosted experience as a destination for whichever traffic you want to test first — a website CTA, Instagram, paid campaign, email, QR, or a featured collection. Longer term, the same merchant can run multiple experiences for different audiences or campaigns while keeping the same catalog and intelligence layer.

### Internal interpretation

```text
Merchant
  ↓
Storefront = always-on SaaS surface
  ↓
Campaign A = Summer sunglasses
Campaign B = Professional frames
Campaign C = Paid social traffic
Campaign D = AI-assistant / referral traffic
```

Campaign therefore expands the Storefront; it does not replace it.

## 13. Objection Handling

### “We already have virtual try-on.”

> That can coexist with VisuTry. Our differentiation is the decision journey before and after the image: narrowing your catalog, explaining recommendations, comparing finalists, and measuring product intent. The pilot can tell us whether that layer adds value beyond your current VTO.

### “We already have Shopify.”

> We are not replacing Shopify. Your commerce system remains the source of product and checkout truth. VisuTry is the decision experience between traffic and your product pages. We can validate that through a hosted experience before building deeper integration.

### “We have too many products to upload.”

> The first pilot does not require the full inventory. We start with a representative 8-20 frame set. The product roadmap already supports assisted catalog onboarding and a repeatable CSV path before heavier sync work.

### “Do we need EHR/PMS integration?”

> Not for the first commerce pilot. We intentionally avoid medical, prescription, and clinical workflows in the initial product. The first question is whether recommendation + try-on + compare improves shopper intent.

### “Can it guarantee physical fit?”

> No. VisuTry is visual decision support and recommendation guidance, not a medical, optical, PD, or guaranteed physical-fit measurement product.

### “Can AI agents buy directly?”

> Not in the current pilot. We are structuring merchant/product identity and traffic attribution so AI-assistant traffic can be understood and measured, but autonomous agent checkout is not part of M1.

### “Why not just send people to our product catalog?”

> You should keep your catalog and product pages. VisuTry is for shoppers who need help deciding which products deserve attention first. The pilot measures whether that guided path produces stronger clicks, inquiries, or conversion signals.

## 14. Claims Boundary

Sales materials must not claim currently unsupported capabilities.

### Safe claims

- uses merchant's own catalog;
- personalized frame recommendation;
- virtual try-on;
- frame comparison;
- product click / favorite / inquiry intent;
- merchant-scoped insights;
- anonymous-first shopper flow;
- attribution to source/campaign where available;
- AI-assistant referral classification where reliably identifiable;
- merchant catalog can begin with CSV / assisted onboarding for M1.

### Do not claim yet

- guaranteed conversion uplift;
- guaranteed return reduction;
- medical or optical fit accuracy;
- PD measurement;
- prescription recommendations;
- live inventory unless merchant data supports it;
- autonomous AI-agent purchase;
- public agent API;
- production Shopify sync;
- advanced multi-touch attribution;
- full CRM / marketing automation.

Use “designed to improve / test / measure” before merchant evidence exists; use “improved by X%” only after a controlled merchant case supports it.

## 17. Alignment With Product / Engineering

Sales must not outrun the current execution plan.

Current product foundation already supports:

- merchant tenant / catalog;
- anonymous merchant session;
- recommendation shortlist;
- merchant-attributed Try-On and Compare;
- intent / insight records;
- privacy boundaries;
- source / campaign attribution direction;
- agent-ready product identity direction.

Current sales narrative should therefore emphasize what is real now while testing the next product layers.

Use this sequence:

```text
Sell Storefront
    ↓
Validate real traffic + merchant KPI
    ↓
Learn repeated campaign / audience needs
    ↓
Expand Campaign Engine
    ↓
Build Commerce Intelligence
    ↓
Add deeper distribution / integrations only when repeated demand exists
```

The sales motion is part of product discovery. Merchant objections and pilot behavior should feed directly into the Store implementation gates, not into an unstructured feature backlog.
